Why we restarted

Every fix was bolted onto the same card

The German word Schlimmbesserung means an improvement that makes things worse, and that describes the last card. Each request was added to the card as it came, and nothing was taken away to make room. By the end the card had:

  • a ring, a side camera, a context line, a temperature bar, a food bar and Stop;
  • swipe pages for the idle tiles;
  • oven chips with underline tabs and their own Stop buttons;
  • a second swipe page for the graph, an add-time group beside Stop, and a camera window in the header.

That left two paging systems, a camera that kept shrinking, and no clear order of importance.

This time the process runs the other way. Each state of the oven first gets a budget: its one job, what goes on the card, what is one tap away, and what becomes a notification. Only then is anything drawn. The designers never saw the old study. They worked only from the requirements below and the new research.

Needs and wants

What every concept had to deliver

The full list, each item with where it came from. Must items fail a design if missed. Should items are the project lead’s explicit wants or strongly backed by evidence; a designer may depart from one only by saying why.

Look and platform

  • MustFeels like June’s oven and app, under July’s own identity: no June wordmark, no carrot orange as a brand colour, an open font close to June’s. Lead
  • MustMatch Home Assistant (default), Light, and Dark (the oven’s glass). Lead
  • MustFits a fixed Home Assistant grid box at any section width from 320 to 500 px. Standard, wall-tablet and one-row compact sizes. HA
  • MustWall size readable from 2–3 m, with numerals about 110–150 px. Evidence

Camera

  • MustThe camera is in every design and every size. Lead
  • MustNever inside a temperature ring. Lead
  • MustThe full, uncropped 4:3 frame, labelled with its age. Live video only when expanded, and only for 5 minutes. The camera stays secondary to status. Evidence
  • MustAn honest “Camera off” when idle, with a way to turn it on. Data

Safety and states

  • MustStop takes one tap, is never confirmed, and is always visible while the oven heats. Safety
  • MustStarting is deliberate: two steps with outcome-labelled buttons, and a review that expires after 5 minutes. Safety · UL 1026
  • MustHandles off, preheating, ready, cooking (timer or probe), door open (heating paused), done, and offline. Data
  • MustThe main number is the largest thing on the card. Status is shown in colour, shape and word. Targets are 48 px or larger. Nothing depends on swiping alone. Evidence

Features the lead asked for

  • Should+1, +5 and +10 min buttons while a timer runs. Lead
  • ShouldA temperature graph showing oven, food and door openings together. Door openings come from real door data, never guessed. Lead
  • ShouldAn idle home like the oven’s: a centred clock, and square mode tiles in swipe pages with dots. Lead · June
  • ShouldA Settings tile after the last cook mode. Lead · June
  • ShouldSwitch between several ovens. Ovens that are off look smaller and quieter. A heating oven is never hidden. Lead · Safety

Research

Three new strands, done fresh

June’s glass screen, state by state

  • The hero is always one huge, thin, condensed number: the clock, the time left, or the temperature. Two or three small centred lines sit below it.
  • Two rings. While preheating, a blue-to-red heat-gradient ring with the current temperature inside. While cooking, a flat ember ring that drains as time runs out, with a small “or more” under the number.
  • The home screen is 3 pages of glowing tiles, each lit where its heating elements are. The tiles are slightly wider than square. Settings, Clean and Connect are circles on the last page.
  • Buttons name their outcome (“Start Broiling”, “Keep Roasting”) and stack full-width. June never had +1/+5/+10; extra time came from “Keep Cooking” once the timer ended.
  • June’s published renders show no page dots. The lead has seen them on the real oven, so dots stay as the lead’s account of the device.

What fits on a glanceable surface

  • Every platform says the same thing: one job per surface, only the critical facts, and the rest in a detail view. That covers Apple widgets and Live Activities, Android widgets, Wear OS tiles and Home Assistant’s own dashboards.
  • Apple advises limiting a Live Activity to a single element and allowing at most one or two prominent buttons in a view.
  • The 2-second test, borrowed from the NHTSA’s in-car glance limit: in one glance, do you know the state, the number, and whether you need to act?
  • Swipe pages have weak evidence on dashboards. They are acceptable only for picking a cook mode while idle, with at most 3 pages and a tap alternative.
  • A full graph is analysis and belongs one tap away. A card carries a word-sized line at most.

How comparable products do it

  • Camera ovens (Anova, Miele, GE) show a refreshing still and keep live video one tap away.
  • Every graph lives on its own screen (MEATER, Combustion, Traeger, ThermoWorks). None of them marks door openings on a graph, so ours is new ground.
  • Adding time exists as Breville Joule’s “A Bit More” and as the +1:00 button on the Android clock app’s timer notification. Nobody puts +1/+5/+10 next to Stop.
  • Remote start requires arming at the appliance (Home Connect, Wolf, GE), which matches our safety rules.
  • Tovala users complained when an update hid the cook stage behind a small timer. Calm designs keep things in place rather than moving them.

Method

A budget before a drawing

Every designer wrote this budget for all eight states before drawing anything. It comes from the glanceability research. The slot counts are that research’s synthesis rather than a published standard.

1Main number
1Status: word, shape, colour
≤ 1Time estimate
≤ 2Supporting facts
1Camera still
≤ 1Main button
≤ 1Quiet group of 3 at most
≤ 5Kinds of element

Every requirement gets exactly one home: the card, one tap away in the detail view, or a notification. A bigger size may add one layer but never a new job, and a card has one paging system at most. New requests follow “one in, one out”, and the 2-second test is run again after every change.

Several ovens: all three designers landed in the same place

Working separately, all three chose the same model. One card per oven stays the default, which is how Home Assistant treats devices. An optional multi-oven card then works like this:

  • The focused oven gets the full card. By default it is the first oven that is heating, so the card never opens on an idle oven while another one heats.
  • Every other heating oven gets a compact strip with its number, status and its own Stop, labelled with its name (“Stop Garage”). It is never hidden.
  • An oven that is off becomes a small, quiet chip or row (“○ Kitchen · Off”), with no colour and no highlight.
  • Tapping a strip or chip makes that oven the focus. The strips are the switcher, so there are no tabs and no second paging system.

With two ovens they differ only in placement: strips above the card (Second Screen, Door) or rows below it (Still Glass). Pick Ovens: Two or Three above to compare them.

Three ovens, two of them off. As first drawn, all three designs gave every oven that is off its own row. Second Screen stacks 48 px rows that squeeze the main card. Door stacks its chips and shrinks its door. Still Glass grew to 12 × 8. Still Glass is now revised: every oven that is off shares one row of small pills, and each heating oven keeps a row of its own. Three ovens with only one heating stay at 12 × 7.

Concept 1

Lens: the oven’s screen

Second Screen

The card is a second copy of the June oven’s glass screen, with a window into the oven beside it. It keeps the oven’s two rings (a heat gradient while preheating, and ember draining while cooking), one huge thin number with a small qualifier, and two or three centred lines. Below both sits one action row that never moves: Stop on the right, and the quiet +1/+5/+10 group on the left.

Camera
A 4:3 window beside the ring, never larger than it. When idle, it shrinks to a small “Camera off · Turn on” pane in the corner.
Idle
The oven’s home screen: a centred clock, glowing element tiles on 2 pages with 48 px dots, and a Settings tile with the oven’s grey gear. The wall size shows all 13 tiles on one grid.
Graph
One tap away. The detail view’s Graph page has the oven and food lines, their targets, and door bands drawn only from door data.
+1/+5/+10
Bottom-left, at least 48 px from Stop. When done they become June’s “Keep baking”.
Several ovens
The chosen oven gets the full card. Every other heating oven gets a strip with its own “Stop Garage”. Ovens that are off shrink to a quiet grey row.

Strengths

  • The most unmistakably June: its rings and glyphs are the oven’s own.
  • The camera has a real place, never squeezed and never in the ring.
  • The action row is the same in every state.

Risks and departures

  • The number is smaller: it sits inside the ring, 68 px in a 500 px section and 49 px on a phone.
  • Idle tiles keep a low ember glow at rest, so the card is only partly calm.
  • The rings are custom drawing, so it feels the least native to Home Assistant.
  • When off or done there is no number, so the clock or the word “Done” leads.

Concept 2 · Recommended

Lens: budget first

Still Glass

This concept starts from the budget and adds nothing to it. Every state has the same four fixed places:

  • top left: the oven’s name;
  • one huge, thin number with its status mark and a short qualifier right under it (“↑ Preheating”, “● Cooking · left”);
  • on the right, the camera’s window;
  • top right, one red action exactly as wide as the camera, on the same line as the name.

Revised with the lead:

  • a red Stop above the camera;
  • the status under the number;
  • no repeated “°F” and a smaller degree sign;
  • an up arrow for preheating;
  • dimmed rows for other ovens;
  • the camera’s top lined up with the top of the big number’s degree sign;
  • the flame from Second Screen as the cooking mark, in orange;
  • shorter +1 · +5 · +10 min buttons in the bottom band, so nothing shifts between states;
  • words in Barlow Semi Condensed, the same as Second Screen, with numbers in Barlow Condensed.

Glass comes from material rather than extra elements: a near-black surface with one diagonal sheen, a thin condensed number, and a single heat line that warms from blue to ember and goes dark and dashed when heat stops.

Camera
In the same right-hand place in every running state, uncropped, with its age in a small pill on the picture’s corner (“LIVE”, or “3 min ago” in amber), the same pill as on the other ovens’ rows. When idle, a small “Turn on” window sits in the corner.
Idle
The oven’s first screen: a centred clock and date, and two rows of glowing square tiles. A standard card shows 4 across, on 2 pages with 48 px dots. A wall panel shows 7 across, with all 13 on one page and no dots. The tiles size themselves to fit both ways. Settings is the last tile, in the same shape but grey glass.
Graph
One tap away in the detail view’s Graph page. The wall size adds one word-sized oven line with door marks, its one extra layer.
+1/+5/+10
Short, wide outline buttons along the bottom of the card, only while a timer runs. They sit outside the main group, so the number, status, bar and camera stay at the same height in every state. Each button is drawn 36 px tall with a 48 px tap area. Stop is at the top right, well away from them.
Several ovens
The heating oven gets the full pane. Other heating ovens get a slightly dimmed row with their own red Stop on the right. All ovens that are off share one row of small grey pills (“○ Garage Off”, “○ Studio Off”), so three ovens take at most one extra row between them.

Strengths

  • The largest and fastest number: 109 px in a 500 px section, 93 px on a phone, and 142 px on the wall.
  • The calmest card. Only heat glows, and at most 5 kinds of element in any state.
  • Match HA makes it a normal Home Assistant card with the same places.
  • Passes every must without stretching one.

Risks and departures

  • The least literally June: a heat line, not the oven’s ring.
  • The camera is smaller, about a third of the width.
  • In a probe cook, “131°” may be read as the oven temperature, even with “food °F · target 145°” under it. Test first.
  • Stop at the top right is a new place for it. Test that people still find it in the first glance.

Concept 3

Lens: the oven’s front

Door

The card is the June oven’s black glass door, reduced to its two openings. The window (top left) holds the camera at exact 4:3. The screen (an inset panel on the right) holds the status, one huge thin number, a thin progress line, two short lines, and a red Stop. The metaphor removes things: no header, no tab row, no mode icon. The oven’s name sits under the window like a nameplate.

Camera
The largest of the three: 231 × 173 px in a 500 px section. When idle the window shrinks to a small dark pane that is itself the “Turn on” button.
Idle
The glass shows a June-like home across its full width: a centred clock, and 5 unlit element tiles per page across 3 pages, with ‹ • • • › buttons. Settings is the last tile.
Graph
One tap away. Tapping the number opens the Graph page, which has legend toggles.
+1/+5/+10
Under the window, on the food side of the door, so they are apart from Stop by column, style and height.
Several ovens
The focused oven gets the door. Other heating ovens get a strip with their own Stop. Ovens that are off become 48 px chips.

Strengths

  • The camera has a natural, generous place of its own.
  • The clearest separation between the time buttons and Stop, as the research advises.
  • A smaller footprint: 12 × 5 instead of 12 × 6.
  • 150 px numerals on the wall.

Risks and departures

  • The camera may win the first glance. It is the first and largest thing on the card, while the brief makes status primary (see the audit).
  • The tiles are unlit at rest, so it is less June when idle.
  • On a phone, the dark glass under the window sits empty in states without a timer.

Recommendation

Build Still Glass, and test it against Second Screen

Still Glass

It is the direct answer to why we restarted. It passes every must without stretching any, it reads fastest (the largest number of the three), and it is the calmest. It works as a normal Home Assistant card under Match HA and as the oven’s dark glass under Dark. Its four fixed places mean nothing moves between states, so a new feature can’t quietly add clutter: it has to take one of the existing places or go one tap away.

What it gives up: June’s ring. If the ring is the part of Glass that matters most, Second Screen is the alternative. It is the most June of the three, and the difference in score comes from calm and number size, not from any failed must.

First usability round

  • Test Still Glass against Second Screen, since June feel is where they differ most. Use 5 people per round, at least half aged 65 or over.
  • Run the 2-second test in every state. Do people read the state, the number and whether to act?
  • Test the probe cook first: is “131°” read as food or oven?
  • Stop success must be 100%. Count mis-taps on +1/+5/+10.

Hold these, don’t graft them

  • Door puts the time buttons under the camera, apart from Stop. That is the best separation of the three, and the research backs it.
  • Second Screen’s preheat heat-gradient is June’s own.
  • Neither goes into Still Glass before testing. Grafting parts across concepts is how the last card got worse.

Why not Door

  • It gives the camera the best place, but that is also its risk: the camera takes the first glance, and the brief makes status primary.
  • If testing shows people still read the number first, Door is a strong contender again.

Open questions

What we still need to find out

From the oven

  • Door state. The oven cuts its elements while the door is open, so it knows. june-local has to report it before door bands and “Door open · heating paused” can work.
  • The oven’s own UI. It is inside the oven’s storage image and hasn’t been extracted. Extracting it would settle the door-open, error, stop and time-setting screens, and whether there are page dots.
  • Which modes accept remote start. Only Bake is proven.

For the lead

  • Settings: a tile in the same shape as the modes (your ask), or the oven’s grey circle? All three concepts chose the tile.
  • Tile shape: square (your ask) or the oven’s slightly wider tile? All three chose square.
  • +1/+5/+10 on the compact size: all three left them off to keep Stop clear. Is that acceptable?
  • After done, “Keep baking” heats again. Should it go through the review, as Door proposes, or just add time?

For testing

  • Readability at 2–3 m on a real wall tablet.
  • Whether people find Settings on the last tile page.
  • Whether page dots at 48 px are found and used on a dashboard.
